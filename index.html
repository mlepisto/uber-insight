You don’t need this. The whole board already runs on the schedule the daily job publishes — no key. This only adds to-the-minute delays for today (a flight going late or cancelling after the morning pull), fetched in your browser.

With a key, tapping Update also pulls actual flight numbers and delays (ON TIME / LATE / LANDED) and re-buckets late flights into the window they really land in.

Updates only when you tap Update (or the LIVE badge). Page loads use the cached copy — no API call. One call per Update in the afternoon/evening, two in the early morning.

Usage: the browser can’t read the quota counter (the feed hides it from in-page scripts), so check units used on your RapidAPI dashboard → Analytics.

Setup once (free, no card, ~2 min): make a RapidAPI account, subscribe to AeroDataBox's Basic (free) plan, copy the key, paste it below. Saved on this device only.

How to read it. Each row is a 30-minute window, and the tabs up top switch what fills it. Arrivals buckets flights by when they land (people hitting the curb needing a ride out). Departures buckets by when travelers likely head to the airport — flight time minus the lead you set (2h30m mainland default, an hour less interisland). All flights stacks both into one total-demand curve. Bigger bar, bigger wave. Mainland and widebody are weighted heavier than interisland (visitors with bags and hotel trips beat locals on a short hop). Peak blocks are your best odds; stage before them, not during.

On the data. With a key connected, today runs on the live arrivals feed. Without one, and for future days, the board falls back to a realistic typical OGG timetable — real airlines, routes, aircraft and seats, but approximate times — so treat those as a planning pattern. Cruise calls come from an editable schedule (no free live source exists) — confirm dates on the Maui / Kahului Harbor cruise schedule before banking a cruise day.

Seats are per-aircraft estimates. All times Hawai‘i Standard Time (HST, no DST). Live flight data by AeroDataBox.
